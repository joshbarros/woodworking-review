What you actually get

The product is a digital library. You get a username, a dashboard, and a searchable set of woodworking plans. Each plan follows the same structure, which is the main reason it works for beginners: nothing is left to guess.

Cut lists and material lists

Every plan ships with the board sizes and quantities, so you buy the right lumber once — not three trips to the store.

Step-by-step diagrams

Exploded views and assembly steps rather than a single flat drawing. Measured in imperial with metric conversion on most plans.

Printable PDFs

Print the pages you need and take them to the shop. No phone on the sawdust-covered bench.

Beginner to advanced

From birdhouses and planter boxes to workbenches, benches, beds and full shed builds.

The honest part: what it is not

  • It is not a course. You get plans, not video instruction. If you have never used a saw, start with the simplest projects and expect a learning curve.
  • It is not free plans. Free plans exist and are fine for one-off projects; the trade-off is missing cut lists, inconsistent measurements and dead links.
  • You still buy lumber. The plans do not include materials — obviously.
  • There are upsells. As with most digital products, you will see additional offers after checkout. They are optional; the core library is what you came for.
  • 16,000 is a big number. Most people will use 5 to 20 plans. The value is in finding the right one quickly, not in the count itself.
